# Diction Tuning Skill

Master the art of word origin selection to match sound, weight, and feeling to meaning.

## The Core Principle

English has two vocabularies living in one language:
- **Germanic (Anglo-Saxon)**: the words of the body, the home, the heart
- **Latinate (French/Latin)**: the words of the court, the academy, the church

Neither is inherently better. The art is matching the word to the moment.

## When to Use Germanic Words

### Emotional Intensity
Germanic words hit harder:
- love, hate, fear, dread, wrath, hope
- NOT: affection, animosity, apprehension, consternation, ire, optimism

### Physical Action
Germanic words have weight:
- hit, strike, cut, break, fall, run
- NOT: impact, assault, lacerate, fracture, descend, proceed

### Concrete Description
Germanic words are specific:
- blood, bone, stone, fire, water, skin
- NOT: sanguine fluid, osseous matter, mineral formation

### Dialogue (Especially Informal)
People speak Germanic:
- "Get out." vs. "Please vacate the premises."
- "Help me." vs. "Render assistance."

### Death, Violence, Sex
These demand directness:
- kill (not terminate)
- wound (not injure)
- body (not corpse, unless tone requires it)

## When to Use Latinate Words

### Technical Precision
When Germanic lacks the concept:
- photosynthesis (no Germanic equivalent)
- democracy, constitution, legislation

### Formal Register
Appropriate for:
- Legal documents
- Academic writing
- Official speeches
- Character voice (if they would speak that way)

### Softening or Distance
Sometimes intentionally:
- "passed away" vs "died" (if euphemism is appropriate)
- "terminate employment" vs "fire" (if formality is needed)
- "romantic involvement" vs "affair" (if delicacy matters)

### Abstract Concepts
Some ideas are inherently Latinate:
- justice, liberty, philosophy
- But try to ground them with concrete Germanic examples

### Cases Where Latinate May Be Better

| Germanic | Latinate (if needed) |
|---|---|
| kingly | royal, regal (more common) |
| bookish | literary (different connotation) |
| friendship | amity (formal contexts) |
| understand | comprehend (technical precision) |

## Rewriting Examples

### Example 1: Action Scene

**Latinate-heavy (weak):**
"He observed her departure through the window, experiencing profound consternation as she proceeded toward the vehicle."

**Germanic rewrite (strong):**
"He watched her go. Through the window she walked to the car, and something cold moved in his gut."

### Example 2: Emotional Beat

**Latinate-heavy (weak):**
"She experienced considerable emotional distress upon perceiving his countenance."

**Germanic rewrite (strong):**
"When she saw his face, her heart stopped."

### Example 3: Description

**Latinate-heavy (weak):**
"The illumination penetrated the translucent material, creating a luminous effect."

**Germanic rewrite (strong):**
"Light came through the thin cloth and made it glow."

### Example 4: Keeping Some Latinate

**Over-Germanized (awkward):**
"The folk-meeting chose a new head-man by putting stones in boxes."

**Balanced (better):**
"The assembly elected a new leader by ballot."

(Sometimes the Latinate word is the normal word.)

## Context-Specific Guidance

### Narrative Prose
- Default to Germanic
- Use Latinate for character voice (educated characters)
- Use Latinate for ironic distance
- Ground abstract passages with concrete Germanic details

### Dialogue
- Match character education and background
- Even educated people use Germanic in stress
- "Help!" not "Assistance!"

### Exposition
- Latinate acceptable for technical concepts
- But explain through Germanic examples
- Alternate: Latinate concept, then Germanic illustration

### Poetry (and Poetic Prose)
- Germanic for sound texture (harder consonants)
- Latinate can create flowing passages
- Mix for contrast

## Diagnosis Process

1. **Highlight Latinate words** in a passage
2. **Check each one:** Is there a Germanic alternative?
3. **Test the alternatives:** Does it work in context?
4. **Consider sounds:** Read both versions aloud
5. **Consider meaning:** Does the substitution preserve nuance?
6. **Decide:** Keep original or substitute

## Quick Tests

### The "Could a Child Understand It?" Test
If explaining to a ten-year-old, which word would you use?
That's usually the Germanic one. Use it unless you have reason not to.

### The "Read It Aloud" Test
Which sounds more natural spoken?
- "I need to utilize the facilities" → stilted
- "I need to use the bathroom" → natural

### The "Punch" Test
For moments of high emotion or action, does the word land?
- "He terminated her existence" → thud
- "He killed her" → lands

## Warning Signs

Passage may be over-Latinate if:
- You see multiple -tion, -ment, -ity endings
- The prose feels bureaucratic or academic
- Readers might need a dictionary
- The rhythm feels heavy and slow

Passage may be over-Germanic if:
- It sounds archaic or affected (trying too hard)
- Technical precision is lost
- It reads like parody ("bethinked" instead of "considered")
